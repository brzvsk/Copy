Brand exploration — round 2

Copy — logo concepts

Round 1 didn't land: the abstract ring read as a random shape, the stacked bars read as something unfortunate, and the shelf mark itself was too soft to commit to. This round stays in the shelf/card/clipboard territory that had real potential and pushes six directions much further — bolder silhouettes, a touch of the app icon's own tonal depth (a soft graphite gradient, a quiet contact shadow), still zero color accents.

Top pick

The Clip

The literal clipboard, stripped down: a plain card with a simple flattened tab clamped across the top and a single slot cut into it — no chrome, no teeth, no padded corners, so it doesn't sit anywhere near Paste's trade dress. Closest kin to the existing app icon of the six, which is also its main risk: it can read as a re-crop rather than a new mark.

The Fold

Wildcard

The one genuine swing: a card with its corner dog-eared, revealing the lighter underside of the page. Nothing else in the set uses this move — it borrows the universal "there's a page here" cue instead of the clipboard/shelf vocabulary, and it's the most tactile, premium-feeling shape of the six. Trade-off: it reads as "a note" more than it reads as "your clipboard history," so it leans hardest on the wordmark to close the gap.

Studio recommendation

1 — The Lift. It's the direct answer to "develop the shelf direction further": the open slot in the ledge plus the risen card tells a one-frame story (something left the shelf, right there, a second ago) instead of just describing "cards," which is what made round 1's version feel inert. The soft graphite gradient and contact shadow give it real weight without adding any color.

4 — The Punch is the strongest runner-up and arguably the safer pick if favicon robustness matters more than storytelling: one squircle, one cut-out card, nothing to misread at any size. 2 — The Duplicate is the cleanest execution of "copy" as a concept and would make a fine standalone brandmark independent of the app icon. 3 — The Row is the most literal but the busiest at 16px. 5 — The Clip is honest but sits closest to the existing icon. 6 — The Fold is the real wildcard — worth a look if "premium and a little unexpected" beats "obviously a clipboard."

Every mark here was rendered in an actual browser at both hero and true 16px/32px favicon size before being included — round 1 shipped a mark whose silhouette only became obvious as a problem once it was on screen, so this time nothing made the page without that check. Palette: a soft graphite gradient (#82858E → #26282C on light, #F6F6F4 → #C9CBCF on dark) matching the app icon's own tile colors, plus one flat mid-gray for secondary/background elements. No color accent anywhere. Favicon-scale renders use flat fills (no gradient) since it's imperceptible at that size and it's one less thing to go wrong small.
